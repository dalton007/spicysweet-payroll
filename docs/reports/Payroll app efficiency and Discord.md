# Cut Spicy Sweet payroll to minutes, not hours

**Stop retyping before you redesign anything.** The biggest time sink in your weekly run is almost certainly typing ~14 chatters' hours and sales (and retyping Fansly PPV sales from Discord) by hand, and the fix is a paste-and-preview import plus a Discord `/sale` command that writes sales straight into the app. Those two changes, plus a "review only the flagged rows" screen and a one-click "Publish week" button, should remove most of the manual work. Posting the leaderboard and sending each chatter a paystub on Discord is very doable, but it needs a small server-side piece (a Supabase Edge Function) so your Discord secrets never sit in the public GitHub Pages code ([Discord webhook docs](https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/webhook); [Supabase secrets](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/secrets)). Discord cannot send real money; if you want the app to pay people, the realistic options are a payout file you upload to your payment provider (safe, cheap) or one API-based provider such as PayPal Payouts, with account-closure risk because almost every mainstream payment company restricts adult-industry business ([Discord monetization](https://docs.discord.com/developers/platform/app-monetization.md); [Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses); [Wise](https://wise.com/acceptable-use-policy)). Fansly has no official creator API that I could find, so per-chatter sales must come from chatters reporting them in a structured way, checked against each model's Fansly earnings export. Invoices to models are an easy build (you already make PDFs), but collect payment by bank transfer or similar, not Stripe or PayPal. Everything below is labelled as sourced (a cited page says it) or inference (my reasoning); no source I found measures how long a payroll run takes, so time your own run first. Nothing here is legal or tax advice.

## The payroll run: kill the retyping first

No credible source measures how long a weekly payroll run takes at a small chatter agency, so the time-sink ranking here is **inference from how your process works**, not a measurement. The likely order: retyping hours and sales, chasing missing or disputed numbers, re-checking tier maths and bonuses, producing each paystub in several formats, and writing the Discord announcement. Items one, four and five can be automated. A cheap way to confirm this: add a stopwatch in the app for each stage for one week, then invest where the minutes actually go (inference).

**Paste-in import is the single biggest win (inference built on a sourced tool).** Papa Parse, a small free library, reads text copied from Excel or Google Sheets or an uploaded CSV, auto-detects the separator, and flags rows with the wrong number of columns ([Papa Parse docs](https://www.papaparse.com/docs)). The workflow would be: paste or drop a file, the app guesses which column is the chatter and which is sales or hours, you confirm once, and it shows a preview with matched and unmatched names before you click Apply. The app should remember the column mapping and a name-alias list (Discord name, tool display name, legal name) so week two is one click. One important caution: the sources I found for the chatter-management tools (Infloww, Supercreator, CreatorHero, OnlyMonster) confirm they track per-chatter revenue and activity, and CreatorHero and OnlyMonster confirm CSV or Excel export, but **none publish their column names or a public API** ([CreatorHero](https://help.creatorhero.com/en/articles/10426384-chatter-tracking); [OnlyMonster export](https://docs.onlymonster.ai/management-system/data-export); [Supercreator](https://www.supercreator.app/onlyfans-crm/chatter-management)). So download one real export from whichever tool you use and build the importer around it. Also let the owner choose gross versus net revenue on import, since your $750/$900/$1150 tiers depend on which one the app uses (inference).

**Make the default "copy last week" and enter only the exceptions (inference).** If each chatter has a standard weekly shift pattern, the app can fill hours automatically and you only mark absences and swaps, which is much faster than fourteen manual entries. Hours can also come from a clock-in source: Clockify has a documented API with a Detailed Report endpoint that could total each person's weekly hours ([Clockify docs](https://docs.clockify.me/)), but calling it from a web page risks CORS errors and exposing the key, so it would have to go through an Edge Function. A reported Clockify CORS problem exists on a developer forum, though I only saw its title ([forum thread](https://dev-forum.marketplace.cake.com/t/detailed-reports-api-throws-cors-error-in-prod/93)). A simpler no-tool alternative is a Discord clock-in button or slash command that writes shifts to the database (my idea; I found no source on existing clock-in bots).

**Review only what looks wrong (inference).** No source gives anomaly rules for chatter payroll, so these are suggestions to tune on your own history: hours above what the roster allows, sales with zero hours, sales up or down more than about 60% versus last week, a chatter with no row at all, sales within a few dollars below a tier line, total payout as a share of revenue outside the usual range, and negative net pay after strikes. Show them as a "review" column with a count ("11 ok, 2 warnings, 1 error") and a last-week-versus-this-week difference table, so you look at three rows, not fourteen.

**Finish with one "Publish week" button (inference).** It would lock the week, create every paystub, and post the announcement and leaderboard. Batch-downloading all stubs as one ZIP or one combined PDF is a small change on top of the PDF and PNG exports you already have. A Monday-morning scheduled "draft is ready, 2 flags" reminder to you is possible with Supabase's built-in scheduler, which can call an Edge Function on a timer ([Supabase scheduling](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/schedule-functions)), but keep the final approval a human click.

## Discord: leaderboard and paystubs from the app, and why real money cannot go through it

**The leaderboard is the easy part.** A Discord channel webhook can post text, up to 10 rich embeds, image files and mentions, and it can **edit its own earlier message**, so the leaderboard can update in place all week instead of piling up new posts ([Discord webhook docs](https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/webhook)). The app has to store the id of the message it posted and re-use it (it asks Discord to return that id on the first post). A silent-update flag exists so refreshes do not ping everyone. Discord does not document whether a plain web page may call a webhook; community reports are mixed, and a webhook link in public page code lets anyone spam your channel ([dev.to](https://dev.to/mistval/discord-webhook-powered-contact-form-3lk6)). So the recommended shape (inference) is: the app calls a small Supabase Edge Function that checks you are logged in as owner, then posts using a webhook URL kept in Supabase's secret store ([Supabase secrets](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/secrets)). The function should also block @everyone mentions (inference). Your existing Discord announcement template slots straight in.

**Individual paystubs need a Discord bot, because webhooks only post to channels.** The webhook docs describe only channels and threads, so a private paystub requires a bot (a free Discord "application" you create and invite to your server). The bot can open a direct message with a chatter and post an embed plus a PNG or PDF attachment; files can be up to 20 MiB ([Discord user resource](https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/user.md); [Discord file upload reference](https://docs.discord.com/developers/reference.md)). Two cautions are sourced: Discord warns against using this to DM everyone in a server and says DMs should generally start from a user action, so opening many DMs quickly can get the bot limited ([Discord user resource](https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/user.md)); and community reports say sending fails when a person has DMs turned off or shares no server with the bot (secondary sources only, [Latenode](https://community.latenode.com/t/why-cant-my-discord-bot-send-direct-messages-to-users/14023)). For 14 people that is manageable if you send them one at a time with a short pause and show a per-chatter status (Sent, DMs closed, Failed) with a retry button (inference). A private thread in a staff channel avoids closed DMs but is weaker for privacy, since anyone with access to the thread or with thread-management rights can read it (inference). Because a server function has no screen, your browser should draw the stub to an image and pass it to the function (inference). The Edge Function limits are workable: 2 seconds of CPU and 150 seconds of waiting per call, so send one chatter per call ([Supabase limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/limits.md)).

**Knowing each chatter's Discord ID.** Supabase supports "Sign in with Discord", and the resulting identity's provider id is the user's account id at Discord ([Supabase Discord login](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/social-login/auth-discord); [Supabase identities](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/identities)). Inference: that id is the number the bot needs for DMs and @mentions, so chatters who log in with Discord give it to you automatically; for everyone else, paste it once (Discord Developer Mode, Copy User ID). Take the id from the verified login, never from something a user can edit. Test one sign-in first, because the exact data fields were not confirmed.

**Discord cannot send money.** Discord's only built-in payment feature is for apps selling subscriptions or one-time purchases to users; nothing in it pays people ([Discord monetization](https://docs.discord.com/developers/platform/app-monetization.md)). Discord is useful only as the notification and control surface ("payout batch ready", "paid"). Paying chatters has to happen on a payment rail, and the research found a sharp pattern: nearly every mainstream rail lists adult content or services as prohibited or restricted. Stripe lists adult content and services, including pay-per-view, as prohibited ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)); Wise lists adult content and "webcam shows, live chats" as restricted and says such transactions may be declined ([Wise](https://wise.com/acceptable-use-policy)), and an advocacy group reports Wise cutting off adult-industry creators ([SpankChain](https://www.spankchain.com/spankbyte-collection/wises-antics-and-what-to-do-if-youre-debanked)); Payoneer prohibits "adult live chat features" ([Payoneer](https://www.payoneer.com/legal/prohibited-transaction-list/)); PayPal requires prior approval for adult content but does not flatly ban it ([PayPal AUP](https://www.paypal.com/gb/webapps/mpp/ua/acceptableuse-full)). Those lists target selling adult content, and a back-office agency paying chatters is a gray zone: it is not clear that they apply to you, and enforcement is judgment-based (inference). Paxum is the one option marketed to adult and webcam businesses and has a mass-payout API ([Paxum](https://paxum.com/services/mass-payouts)). PayPal Payouts is the most straightforward technically, with a sender fee around 2% and no fee to recipients ([PayPal fees](https://developer.paypal.com/payouts/fees.md)); Trolley is built for contractor payouts and tax forms but its pricing was not found ([Trolley](https://trolley.com/blog/trolley-vs-tipalti-vs-payoneer-which-is-best-for-paying-gig-workers)). Fees, speeds and country coverage were not verified for most rails.

My recommendation (inference) is a ladder. Step one: a "payout file" export (name, email or account, amount, reference = week number) that you upload to whatever provider you already use, with a manual "mark paid" box; this carries no new provider risk and is about a day of work. Only if that is still too slow, add one API rail behind a "Pay all" button with a preview, a two-step confirmation, a protection against paying the same person twice in a week, and keys held only in Supabase secrets. Keep a second rail and a week or two of buffer funds, because account closure is the main danger. One compliance flag: moving other people's money from your own app can raise money-transmitter questions, whereas paying from your own funded account to your own contractors is the lower-risk framing; ask a lawyer. Do not let a Discord button approve real-money payouts without strong signature checks.

## Fansly PPV sales: make Discord the entry form, then check it against Fansly

**There is no clean automatic feed.** I found no evidence of an official Fansly creator or agency API and no native per-chatter earnings report. The Fansly earnings data that is reachable is per model: a browser extension, FBuddy, downloads Fansly Wallet History as CSV with date, status (Pending, Approved, Canceled, Refunded), order number, description (tips, subscription, media, bundle and so on) and total, but **no chatter column** ([FBuddy](https://docs.fbuddy.net/features/download-wallet-history-csv)). OnlyMonster and Usly export Fansly account revenue to CSV or Excel but do not break it down by chatter ([OnlyMonster Fansly](https://docs.onlymonster.ai/management-system/accounts-overview-fansly); [Usly](https://intercom.help/usly-1422d0f4f655/en/articles/14470513-creator-analytics)). OnlyMonster's per-chatter attribution rules are documented as OnlyFans-only, though its page contradicts itself on Fansly ([OnlyMonster chatter metrics](https://docs.onlymonster.ai/management-system/chatter-metrics)). Unofficial scrapers exist, but they cover public profiles, and logging into a creator's account through automation means sharing credentials with unquantified ban risk, so I rank it last (inference; Fansly's own terms were not read).

**Best fix: a `/sale` command in Discord.** Supabase publishes an official example of an Edge Function acting as a Discord bot with no always-on server, verifying Discord's signature on each request ([Supabase Discord bot guide](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/examples/discord-bot.md)). Reading free-text posts like "Anna $120" needs a restricted Discord permission (the Message Content intent), but slash commands, right-click message commands, buttons and pop-up forms work without it, and slash commands can carry a screenshot attachment ([Discord guidance](https://docs.discord.com/developers/gateway/you-might-not-need-a-privileged-intent.md)). The design (inference): chatters type `/sale amount:120 model:[pick from list] screenshot:[attach]`; Discord tells the app who they are, so there is no guessing from a name; the sale lands in the app as **pending**; you or a head chatter approve. A right-click "Log sale" on an existing screenshot post lets a head chatter capture older posts. Your payroll then sums approved rows, so the retyping disappears and "who posted this" is never in doubt. If it is easier, a "Log sale" form in the app writes to the same table, so Discord is just one entry route. A Make or Zapier bridge needs your own bot with the restricted intent, polls rather than reacts instantly, and some community users report the trigger not firing ([Make docs](https://apps.make.com/discord.md); [Make community](https://community.make.com/t/discord-watch-channel-messages-is-not-triggering/12071.md)); I would skip it.

**Reconcile and keep honesty cheap (inference).** Add an owner-only Reconcile screen: upload each model's Fansly CSV, compare against approved chatter sales for the week, and show matched sales, chatter claims with no match (typo or inflation) and Fansly sales nobody claimed. Matching one sale at a time catches inflated single entries that totals would miss. Normalize first, since the CSV uses the device's local time and I could not confirm whether amounts are before or after Fansly's fee, and exclude Canceled and Refunded rows. Require a screenshot, and flag a duplicate screenshot or an identical model, time and amount. Reading amounts off screenshots with Tesseract.js is feasible in a browser but unreliable (decimal points and currency symbols are commonly misread, and I found no Fansly-specific accuracy data), so use it only to pre-fill a number the chatter confirms ([ImageToTable](https://imagetotable.ai/blog/ocr-accuracy-by-field-type)). A trick of mine that no source documents: give each chatter a distinctive cents value on PPV prices, as OnlyMonster does for attribution on OnlyFans, so a sale can be traced to a chatter.

## Invoices to models: build from data you already have

**Show the money as a simple waterfall (inference on sourced norms).** Agencies commonly take a percentage of the model's net earnings after OnlyFans' 20% cut, roughly 20-50% for chatting only and up to about 70% for full management; this comes from an agency marketing blog, so it is indicative ([Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/onlyfans-agency-commission-rates)). The same source notes half the market quotes on gross and half on net, often without saying which, and a rate on gross is worth 1.25 times the same rate on net, so **print the base on every invoice** (gross sales, minus platform fee, equals net, times your percentage, equals your fee). The app already holds those figures. Add optional lines for a flat retainer, pass-through costs (ads, tools) with receipts, and credits or corrections.

**Check the direction of money before building.** I found no source on whether your arrangement is "model pays agency" (an invoice) or "agency collects, pays the model" (a statement). Tax treatment differs, so confirm with your contract and an accountant. Either way, collect a W-9 from each US model. The 1099-NEC threshold rises from $600 to $2,000 for payments made from January 1, 2026, with filings due January 31, per a secondary source; verify on irs.gov ([1-800Accountant](https://1800accountant.com/blog/irs-1099-reporting-changes-2026)). Sales tax on management services was not researched; ask the accountant.

**Generate, send, track (inference, with sourced pieces).** Make invoices with the PDF tool you already bundle, number them in a gapless sequence (INV-2026-0001), and make them unchangeable once issued, using a new invoice or a credit note for fixes. Store the PDF in a private Supabase Storage folder and track status (draft, sent, paid, void). Email delivery through Resend is documented with Supabase: the free plan allows 100 emails per day and 3,000 per month, attachments up to 40MB, one invoice per call ([Resend limits](https://www.resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/account-quotas-and-limits.md); [Resend attachments](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/attachments); [Supabase email example](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/examples/send-emails)). The same Discord bot can DM a model a private link instead (inference). Add a "mark paid" button and an overdue reminder, and an Excel export for your bookkeeper rather than a live accounting sync.

**Collecting payment is where the risk is.** Stripe says it cannot work with businesses that sell or offer adult content or services and its list is not exhaustive; whether a management agency counts is not addressed ([Stripe FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/prohibited-and-restricted-business-list-faqs)). PayPal prohibits sexually oriented digital content ([PayPal HELP384](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/HELP384)), and QuickBooks Payments lists adult content and services for merchant processing ([Intuit AUP](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/payments/legal/TOC102017/acceptable-use/)). Treat the invoice as a document, list bank transfer or another method you already use for payment, and avoid depending on a card-payment link (inference).

## Other improvements worth making, and the build plan

**Reliability comes before looks (inference; no competitor source).** For a money app with owners, managers and head chatters all editing: lock a week once approved (with a logged reopen and corrections as adjustment lines in the next week), keep an audit log of who changed what, record paid or unpaid per chatter per week, back up automatically, and warn when someone else changed the week you have open. Add an acknowledge button and a "question this line" note on each paystub for a paper trail; pay transparency suits an industry where press coverage describes low-paid chatters ([Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/onlyfans-management-agency-chatters)). Email "your pay is ready" is a documented pattern ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/examples/send-emails)); iPhone push alerts work only if chatters install the app to their Home Screen and tap to allow them ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/?p=13878)), so do email or Discord first. On quality: a source recommends managers score 10-20 sampled conversations per chatter weekly and warns that rewarding raw volume hurts retention ([remove.tech](https://www.remove.tech/creator-blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-agency-scaling-how-do-i-measure-chatter-performance-across-my-agency-effectively)), so a manager-entered 1-5 quality score that gates the podium and golden-ratio prizes is a sensible, low-cost addition (inference). Per-model sales tagging unlocks per-model profit, which matters because margins are thin: one source's example leaves about €100 for chatting on a 5% commission of a €5,000 month ([Desirely](https://www.desirely.co/en/blog/10-mistakes-onlyfans-agencies-fail)). Streaks, badges and an annual earnings statement for each chatter are cheap extras (inference).

## What is sourced and what is inference

Sourced: Discord webhook, edit and DM mechanics and limits; Supabase secrets, scheduling and Edge Function limits; Discord Interactions via Edge Function; the Message Content intent rule; payment-provider restrictions on adult business; PayPal Payouts fee structure; FBuddy, OnlyMonster, Usly and CreatorHero export capabilities; Resend limits; the 1099-NEC threshold (secondary source); commission norms (agency blog). Inference: every time-saving estimate, the validation rules, the sales-approval workflow and fraud checks, reconciliation matching, invoice layout and numbering, the audit and lock features, all effort figures, and the build order. Not found: column layouts of any chatter-tool export, any official Fansly API, any measure of payroll time, fees and speeds for most payout rails, Fansly's terms on automation, and whether a plain web page may call Discord webhooks.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question: your bottleneck is data entry, not appearance, and the highest-leverage change is making Discord the structured front door for sales (`/sale`) while keeping you as approver. The app already holds the ingredients for paystubs, leaderboards and invoices, so the new work is mostly plumbing and one secure server-side function. Money movement is the one area where a faster feature could hurt you: the payment-rail restrictions are real, and an app that moves funds adds compliance exposure for a small gain, so keep payouts as a file you upload until volume or pain justifies more.

Before building, do three cheap checks: download one real export from your chatter tool and one Fansly Wallet CSV to confirm the columns, confirm with your accountant who invoices whom and what to file for US and overseas chatters, and ask your payment provider in writing whether your business type is acceptable.
